# Raj Shamani-Style Carousel Reference

Observed from the provided circadian-trough PDF.

## Canvas

- Use 4:5 portrait slides, preferably 1080 x 1350.
- Background is white with a pale gray grid, like notebook or graph paper.
- Keep generous margins. Most text blocks sit in the top or middle third with lots of air.
- Add a thin black header rule near the top and a thin footer rule near the bottom.

## Recurring Chrome

- Top left: small uppercase category such as `FIGURING OUT HUMANS`.
- Top right: small uppercase author/brand label such as `RAJ SHAMANI`.
- Bottom left: tiny rounded `SWIPE <<` pill.
- Bottom right: small yellow circular page number.

## Typography

- Use a clean sans font: Arial, Helvetica, Inter, or a similar neutral face.
- Main cover headline: large, black, tight but readable.
- Body copy: small, editorial, sentence-case, no marketing voice.
- Use short paragraphs of 1-3 lines. Avoid centered body paragraphs except on the cover.

## Color

- Primary: black text on white.
- Secondary: soft grayscale visuals.
- Accent: highlighter yellow `#fff200` or `#f7e600`.
- Grid/rules: light gray `#e8e8e8` and black `#111`.

## Visual Language

- Use grayscale cutout photos, minimal black line icons, or simple monochrome illustrations.
- Add yellow marker circles, underlines, loose arrows, and small loops around key words.
- Yellow annotations should feel hand-drawn: imperfect curves, informal arrows, short strokes.
- Avoid glossy gradients, colorful illustrations, drop-heavy cards, and UI-like panels.

## Slide Rhythm

1. Hook: question or surprising claim, one yellow circle/underline, one strong human visual.
2. Setup: everyday situation, then name the concept with a yellow underline.
3. Mechanism: explain why it happens with 2-3 small callouts and one diagram/icon.
4. Example: make the insight concrete with a familiar situation.
5. Action: give a simple reset or behavior change.

## Writing Rules

- Use the structure: observation -> name -> mechanism -> example -> action.
- Keep each slide under about 55 words; cover under 30 words.
- Prefer plain phrases like "Your focus is gone" over abstract phrasing.
- End with a practical move the reader can do today.
